Privacy Policy

This policy explains how Vaulty handles information. Vaulty is published by Flutteroid. The copy inside the app is the policy that applies to your installed version.

Vaulty does not run an account system and does not upload your vault to us. Documents, cards, and passwords stay on your device unless you choose to export, share, or back them up.

What Vaulty stores on this device

When you create a vault, the app stores this information in app storage on the phone:

  • The name you ask us to call you.
  • Your documents, photos of documents, cards, boarding passes, expiry dates, and reminder choices.
  • A master password check and encryption keys. The master password itself is not saved in plain text.
  • Your recovery key, sealed inside the encrypted vault so you can view it again after you enter the master password. Keep your own copy. Flutteroid does not keep one.
  • Settings such as theme, auto-lock, biometric unlock, and screenshot blocking.
  • Whether the one-time unlock is active on this phone. That flag sits outside the vault, so a backup file does not carry it.

Page images are encrypted before they are written to storage. The vault file is encrypted with a key that is wrapped by your master password and, separately, by your recovery key.

What we do not collect

  • No Vaulty account, and no sign-in with Google, Apple, or anyone else.
  • No advertising identifier, no ads, and no analytics about how you use the app.
  • No sale of your information, and no sharing of your vault with Flutteroid.
  • No microphone recording. Vaulty does not record audio.
  • No payment card details. Google Play or the App Store bills the one-time unlock. Vaulty does not receive the card number.

Camera and files

The camera is used only when you scan a document, card, or boarding pass. Scanning and text recognition run on the device. Vaulty does not send those images to a Flutteroid server.

When you import a file, the system file picker opens and you choose what to add. When you save a backup or a recovery key, you choose the destination. Vaulty does not browse your photo library in the background.

Fingerprint and face unlock

If you turn on biometric unlock, the operating system checks your fingerprint or face. Vaulty never receives the biometric image. It only learns whether the check succeeded, and then unlocks a key stored in the device keystore.

Sharing you start

Nothing is shared until you do it:

  • You can share a copy of a document through the system share sheet. A watermark can be added on that copy.
  • You can send a boarding-pass image to DigiYatra. DigiYatra is a separate app with its own policy.
  • You can copy a card detail or the recovery key. Vaulty clears that clipboard entry after about 30 seconds if it is still there.
  • You can save an encrypted backup file and move it yourself to a computer, pen drive, or SD card.
  • You can send a document to the Vaulty app on a computer on the same Wi-Fi. That copy goes from this phone to that computer.

Device backup

Vaulty does not offer cloud sync. Android backup and Android device-to-device transfer are turned off for this app, so the encrypted vault is not copied by them. A backup file you export, a recovery-key file you save, or a full copy of the phone made by a computer can still hold a copy. Those copies stay wherever you put them.

Security

You can block screenshots and hide the vault in the app switcher. Auto-lock closes the vault after a period you choose, and when the screen turns off if that option is on. No method of storage is perfect. Protect the master password and the recovery key. If both are lost, the vault cannot be opened, and Flutteroid cannot reset it.

Children

Vaulty is not directed at children under 13. Do not use it to store a child’s personal documents unless you are the parent or guardian and the law allows it.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new text will ship inside an app update and the date at the top will change. Using the updated app means you accept the updated policy.
